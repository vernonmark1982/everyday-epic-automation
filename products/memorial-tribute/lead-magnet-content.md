## Page 2 — How to Use This

You received this because you needed it quickly, at a hard time. So here is exactly what to do:

1. **Fill in everything in [brackets].** If you don't know a detail, leave it out. A tribute that says something true is more powerful than one that fills every field.
2. **You need one memory.** Not a full biography — one specific memory that shows who they were. If it makes you smile through it, it's the right one.
3. **Read it out loud once before the service.** Not to memorize — just so your voice has met the words before the room does.
4. **It's okay to read from the page.** Nobody at a service has ever thought less of a person for holding a piece of paper. Bring the paper.

This template works read as-is. If you have more strength and time later, personalize it further. Both versions honor them.

## Page 3 — The Eulogy Template

*Approx. 3 minutes read aloud. For any relationship.*

---

We are here today because [Name] mattered — to every person in this room, in ways both large and quiet.

I'm not going to try to summarize [his/her/their] whole life in a few minutes. No one could. Instead, I want to tell you what it was like to be loved by [him/her/them].

[Name] was my [mother / father / husband / wife / sister / brother / friend of number years]. And if you knew [him/her/them], you knew [one quality everyone in the room will recognize: "she never once let you leave hungry" / "he showed up — every time, for everyone" / "they could make you laugh on your worst day"].

Let me tell you one memory.

[The memory. 4–6 sentences. Set the scene in one sentence. What happened in two or three. What it showed about them in one. If your voice breaks while telling it, pause, breathe, and continue — the room will wait with you.]

That was [Name]. Not the dates, not the titles — that. The way [he/she/they] made people feel. The measure of a life isn't written in what someone achieved. It's written in the people who show up when it ends. Look around this room.

[Optional, if it fits your beliefs:] We believe [Name] is at peace now / has gone home / lives on in every one of us. [Choose the line that is true for your family, or skip this entirely.]

So I won't say goodbye. I'll say what [he/she/they] would have wanted us to hear: thank you — for every day we had. We were lucky. We knew it then, and we know it now.

Rest now, [Name]. We'll take it from here.

## Page 4 — The Short Obituary Template

*Approx. 120 words. For a program insert, announcement, or to hand to the funeral home tonight.*

---

[Full Name] — [birth date] – [death date]

[First Name] of [city, state] passed away on [date] at the age of [age].

[He/She/They] was a beloved [mother / father / spouse / sibling / friend] and [one line about what defined them: "a nurse of 30 years" / "the anchor of every family gathering" / "a friend to everyone who crossed her path"].

[First Name] is survived by [list of survivors]. [He/She/They] [was preceded in death by (names), if applicable].

A [memorial service / celebration of life / funeral service] will be held on [date] at [time], at [location]. [Optional: In lieu of flowers, the family requests (donations to / privacy / cards).]

We are better for having known [First Name]. We will not forget.

## Page 5 — If the Loss Was Sudden

If this death was unexpected, some of the language above may feel wrong. That's because sudden loss is a different kind of grief, and the words need to hold it differently.

Swap the opening for this:

*"We are here today because the world changed without warning. None of us were ready — because there was no way to be ready. So we're going to do the only thing we can do: hold on to each other, and remember [Name] the way [he/she/they] deserves to be remembered."*

And know this: at a service after a sudden loss, nobody expects polish. They expect presence. Standing up at all is the tribute. The words are just the vehicle.
