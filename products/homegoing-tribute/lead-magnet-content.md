## Page 2 — How to Use This Template

You received this because you needed it quickly.

This template is designed to be completed in 5 minutes and handed to a funeral home, printed for a program, or read at a service. It works as-is — with your details filled in. It also works as a starting point if you have more time to personalize.

**Three versions are included:**

1. **The Standard Homegoing Obituary** — For an adult who lived a full life. Opens with celebration, not loss. Closes with the certainty of faith.
2. **The Elder Homegoing Obituary** — For a grandparent, matriarch, patriarch, or elder whose life shaped a community.
3. **The Short Program Insert** — 100 words for a program bulletin or memorial card when space is limited.

**Fill in everything in [brackets].** If you don't know a detail — a middle name, a specific date — leave it out. A tribute that says something true is more powerful than one that fills every field.

## Page 3 — Version 1: The Standard Homegoing Obituary

*Approx. 280 words. For program bulletin or reading aloud.*

---

On [date], God called home [Full Name], beloved [mother / father / sister / brother / husband / wife / friend] of [family member names], and a person whose presence in this world was a gift we did not deserve and cannot replace.

We come today not in sorrow alone, but in celebration — of a life lived fully, a faith held firmly, and a love that does not end at the door of this sanctuary.

[First Name] was born on [birth date] in [city, state] to [parent names]. [He/She/They] grew up [brief note about upbringing — city, church, community, or leave blank]. From an early age, [he/she/they] carried [a quality — warmth / a gift for laughter / a quiet strength / a fierce love for family] that those who knew [him/her/them] recognized immediately and never forgot.

[First Name] lived a life that touched [the people around him/her/them] in ways that cannot be reduced to dates and titles. [He/She/They] [loved deeply / worked hard / gave freely / taught by example / showed up for everyone who needed them]. The measure of who [he/she/they] was is not in what [he/she/they] achieved, but in what [he/she/they] left in the people who loved [him/her/them].

[He/She/They] is survived by [list of survivors: children, grandchildren, siblings, spouse, close family and community members].

We send [First Name] home today knowing that this is not goodbye. It is "until we meet again." The work [he/she/they] did here is done. The rest is already handled.

*[First Name] — [birth year] – [death year]. Rest now.*

## Page 4 — Version 2: The Elder Homegoing Obituary

*Approx. 320 words. For a grandparent, matriarch, patriarch, or community elder.*

---

On [date], [Full Name] — known to most simply as [family name: Mama / Big Mama / Grandaddy / Pop / Deaconess / Elder / Sister] — was received into the arms of the God [she/he/they] served for [number] years.

[She/He/They] did not simply live a life. [She/He/They] built one — with her/his/their hands, her/his/their faith, and a kind of love that didn't announce itself but was there every time it was needed.

[First Name] was born on [birth date] in [city, state], the [child / daughter / son] of [parent names]. [She/He/They] came up in a time when Black families were not given much and were expected to build everything from what they had. [She/He/They] built everything from what they had.

For [number] years, [she/he/they] was a [wife / husband / mother / father / grandmother / grandfather / deacon / deaconess / cornerstone of this community]. [She/He/They] [raised her/his children to know who they were / kept a house that anyone could walk into / held this family together in ways we only fully understand now / gave what she/he had before she/he had enough to give].

There is no way to summarize a [life like hers / life like his] in the space given here. What we can say is this: the room is different when someone like [First Name] leaves it. Quieter. Larger in the wrong way. We are all, in some way, the people [she/he/they] shaped us to be.

[She/He/They] is preceded in death by [names] and is survived by [full list of survivors].

We lay [First Name] down today with gratitude, with grief, and with the sure knowledge that the work [she/he/they] came to do here is complete.

*Go home, [First Name / family name]. You earned it.*

*[Full Name] — [birth year] – [death year].*

[Full Name] — [birth date] – [death date]

On [date], [First Name] went home to rest.

We gather today not to say goodbye but to mark the beginning of [her/his/their] rest and to carry forward what [she/he/they] gave us while [she/he/they] was here.

We are better for having known [First Name]. We will not forget.

## Page 6 — Notes and Full Kit

**Personalizing These Templates**

The bracketed sections are starting points. The best tributes add one specific true thing — a habit, a phrase, a moment — that belongs only to this person. If you can add one of those, add it. If you can't find the words right now, the template holds on its own.

**For Sudden Loss**

If this death was unexpected, the tone shifts. Replace "We come today in celebration" with "We come today because we have to — because the world changed without warning, and we are still finding our footing." The rest of the template still applies. Grief and homegoing are not opposites.
