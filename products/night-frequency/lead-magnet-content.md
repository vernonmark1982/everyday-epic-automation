### Page 3 — The Blackout Routine (before you press play)

Music can invite your body to rest. It can't undo a room full of daylight and noise. Set the room first:

**Light is the enemy. Kill it completely.**
- Blackout curtains are non-negotiable for daytime sleep. Not "dark" curtains — blackout. If you can see your hand clearly, it's too bright.
- Cover or remove every LED in the room — chargers, TVs, smoke detectors. A single blue pinpoint tells your brain it's daytime.
- A sleep mask covers what the curtains miss. Cheap insurance.

**Temperature drops the body into sleep.**
- Set the room cooler than feels comfortable when you lie down — the body's core temperature has to fall to initiate sleep, and a warm daytime room fights that.
- 65–68°F (18–20°C) is the common target. Cooler than your nighttime instinct.

**Sound is a wall, not a silence.**
- Total silence lets daytime sounds (mail, mowers, doors) spike through. The music is the wall — start it before you lie down and let it run.
- Set it to loop or queue the full collection so it doesn't stop and leave a gap of daytime noise.

### Page 4 — The Cortisol-Reset Timing

Cortisol — the wake-up hormone — is highest in the morning, which is exactly when a night-shift worker is trying to sleep. You can't switch it off, but you can time around it.

**The 90-minute wind-down window.**
- Don't go straight from the shift to the bed. The drive home, the bright gas station, the phone scroll — all of it keeps cortisol up.
- Give yourself a 60–90 minute descent: dim everything, sunglasses on the drive home if it's bright, no bright screens, and start Blackout as the signal that the descent has begun.

**Eat down, not up.**
- A heavy meal right before daytime sleep spikes alertness. A light snack is fine. Caffeine has a long tail — the coffee at hour 6 of your shift is still in your system at 10 AM.

**Anchor the schedule.**
- The single biggest lever in shift-worker sleep is consistency. Same sleep window every day you work, as close as life allows, so the body stops fighting a moving target.
