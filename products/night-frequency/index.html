Why It Works When Other Sleep Music Doesn't

Daytime sleep is a
different fight. This music
was built for that fight.

When you sleep at night, your body is on your side — melatonin is up, cortisol is down, the light is gone. When you sleep in the daytime after a night shift, every one of those is working against you. Your cortisol is climbing, the sun is up, and your nervous system is convinced it's time to be awake.

Generic sleep playlists ignore all of that. They're lo-fi loops and white noise made for nighttime bedrooms. Night Frequency is engineered — deliberately — for the daytime sleep problem, with a specific acoustic architecture aimed at helping the body come down when biology says it shouldn't.

Tempo
60–72 BPM

Slow enough to invite the resting pulse to follow it down. Every track descends rather than holds — the tempo itself is a wind-down.

Harmonic Contour
Descending

Melodies fall rather than climb. No rising phrases that pull attention back up. The music only ever goes down toward rest.

Frequency Ceiling
No peaks > 4kHz

The bright, alerting frequencies that snap you awake are rolled off. Nothing in the mix says "pay attention."

The DNA
Jazz & soul

Not white-noise ambient. Warm Rhodes, upright bass, brushed textures — music with a spine, not a hum. The OBSIDIAN HOUR sound.

These are the design targets the music was built to. They describe how the tracks were engineered — not a medical claim or a promise of results. Sleep is individual. This is a tool, not a cure.
