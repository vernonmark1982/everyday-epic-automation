# PART FOUR — THE "FUNNY WITHOUT BOMBING" HUMOR GUIDE

The line between the room roaring and the room going silent is thinner than it looks. Here's exactly where it is.

### The Three Rules of Wedding Humor

**Rule 1 — Roast up to a wall, then turn to warmth.** The best man/maid of honor speech has a signature move: three light jabs, then a hard pivot to sincerity. The humor buys you the room's attention; the sincerity is what they actually remember. Never end on a joke. End warm, always.

**Rule 2 — Inside jokes die in big rooms.** If a story requires context that only five people have, it fails for the other 145. Every joke must land for a stranger who's never met the couple. Test it: would your seatmate's grandmother understand why it's funny? If no, cut it.

**Rule 3 — Punch at yourself, not at them.** Self-deprecation is safe and likable. Roasting the couple works ONLY when it's obviously affectionate and clearly ends in a compliment. When in doubt, make yourself the fool, not the newlyweds.

### The Always-Off-Limits List

Never joke about, reference, or "lightly" bring up:
- **Exes.** Any of them. Ever. Not even "remember [ex]?" as a bit. Instant room death.
- **Cheating, doubts, or "I didn't think it would last."** Even ironically.
- **Money, weight, or physical appearance.**
- **How much anyone drank, drinks, or used to drink.**
- **Anything the couple hasn't already told both families.** If a story would reveal something private to a parent, it's a landmine.
- **The cost of the wedding, the food, the venue, or the gifts.**
- **Anything political or religious that isn't clearly shared by the room.**
- **The bachelor/bachelorette party.** Whatever happened, it stays off the microphone.

### The Roast-to-Toast Pivot (the move that makes it land)

Structure your funny sections like this:
1. **Set-up:** an affectionate flaw everyone recognizes ("[Groom] is never on time").
2. **Escalate:** one or two quick examples, building.
3. **The turn:** a single sentence that flips it — "But the day he met [Partner], he was early. First time in his life." Now the joke *is* the compliment.
4. **Land warm:** ride that turn straight into the sincere close.

Get this structure right and you don't need to be a comedian. The shape does the work.

# PART FIVE — THE DELIVERY GUIDE

For people who don't speak in public and would rather not start now.

### The Night Before
- Practice out loud, twice. Not in your head — out loud. The speech that reads well and the speech that *speaks* well are different, and you find the difference with your mouth.
- Time it. Aim for 3–4 minutes. If you're over 5, cut from the middle, never the ending.
- Print it in large type, or put it in your phone with big font. Reading is not cheating. Rambling is worse than reading.

### One Hour Before
- One drink maximum before you speak. The second is your reward, after.
- Eat something. Nerves on an empty stomach are worse.
- Find the couple and tell them you're about to speak, so they're ready to look at you.

### At the Microphone
- **Stand still.** Plant your feet. The urge to sway is real — resist it.
- **Speak 20% slower than feels natural.** What feels too slow to you sounds correct to the room.
- **Look at the couple during the sincere parts. Look at the room during the funny parts.**
- **If a joke dies, keep moving.** Never explain it, never apologize. The room forgets in three seconds if you do.
- **Breathe at the [brackets].** Every paragraph break is a place to breathe. Use them.
- **The last line is the toast.** Raise the glass, say the line, drink. Done. Do not add a bonus ending. The glass going up is the signal — the room will follow you.

### If You Feel Yourself Getting Emotional
- Pause. Breathe. Look down at the page for a second. The room will wait with you — a pause reads as sincerity, not failure.
- Take a sip of water. It's a socially invisible reset.
- Keep going. You don't have to be composed. You have to be honest, and you already are.

# PART SIX — THE "ASKED TO SPEAK TONIGHT" EMERGENCY SECTION

Surprise toasts happen — at the rehearsal dinner, when the DJ hands you the mic, when someone says "say a few words!" Here are three you can personalize in the hallway in 60 seconds.

### Emergency Toast 1 — The 30-Second Save
"Hi everyone, I'm [name]. I wasn't planning to speak, but I'm not going to pass up the chance to say this: [Couple], we love you, we're proud of you, and we're so happy to be here. To the happy couple."

### Emergency Toast 2 — The Rehearsal Dinner
"I want to thank [hosts] for bringing us all together tonight, and thank [Couple] for giving us a reason to. Tomorrow's the big day — but tonight, in this room, with these people, is pretty perfect too. To [Couple], and to everyone who loves them."

### Emergency Toast 3 — The Warm One-Liner (personalize the middle)
"I'm [name], and I've known [Name] for [number] years. In all that time, I've never seen [him/her/them] as happy as [he/she/they] is right now with [Partner]. That's all any of us wants for the people we love. To [Couple] — congratulations, and cheers."

**The emergency formula, if you have to build your own:** Who you are → one true sentence about the couple → raise the glass → "To the happy couple." That's it. Under 30 seconds beats brilliant-but-long every time.
