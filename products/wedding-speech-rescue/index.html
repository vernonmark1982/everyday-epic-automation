Why the Page Is Still Blank

Everyone will be watching.
And you only get one take.

A wedding speech is the highest-pressure three minutes most people ever perform. There's no rehearsal audience, no second chance, and the whole room is holding a phone camera. The reason you haven't written it isn't laziness. It's that the stakes are real and nobody ever taught you the structure.

The Blank Page Problem

You know the stories. You don't know which one to tell, how to open, or how to get from "funny story" to "sincere toast" without the gears grinding. Structure is the missing piece — and structure is exactly what a script gives you.

The Humor Trap

The line between "the room roars" and "the room goes quiet" is thinner than it looks. Inside jokes die in big rooms. Exes are radioactive. The kit's humor calibration guide marks exactly where the line is — so you're funny without bombing.

The Length Problem

Under 2 minutes reads as not caring. Over 6 minutes loses the room. Every script in the kit is built to a 3–4 minute delivery, timed, with cut points marked if you need it shorter.

The Nerves Problem

Shaking hands, dry mouth, racing heart — normal, and manageable with the right prep. The delivery guide covers the 24-hour countdown: what to practice, what to eat, what to do with your hands, and where to look.

Every script in this kit already solved these problems. You add the names and one true story. The structure does the rest.
