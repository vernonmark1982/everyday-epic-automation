## Page 2 — How to Use This Template

You received this because the wedding is close and the page is blank. Good news: you're 5 minutes of filling in blanks and two practice runs away from done.

**The rules before you start:**

1. **Fill in every [bracket].** If a line doesn't fit your relationship, cut it. A shorter speech that's all true beats a longer one that's padded.
2. **You need exactly ONE story.** Not three. One story about the person you're speaking for that shows who they are. Funny is great. True is mandatory. If you're torn between stories, pick the one that ends warm, not the one that ends embarrassing.
3. **Practice out loud, twice.** Once tonight, once tomorrow. Out loud is not optional — the speech that reads well and the speech that speaks well are different speeches, and you find the difference with your mouth, not your eyes.
4. **Time it.** This template delivers in about 3 minutes at a natural pace. If you're over 5, cut the middle, never the ending.

## Page 3 — The Template

*Fill in the brackets. Cut what doesn't fit. This structure works for best man, maid of honor, parent, or sibling.*

---

**[THE OPENER]**

"Good evening, everyone. For those who don't know me, I'm [your name], and I've had the privilege of being [Name]'s [brother / sister / best friend / father / mother / partner in crime] for [number] years.

Which means I've seen some things tonight's couple would probably prefer stayed private — and I paid good money for this microphone."

*(Pause. Let the laugh land. If no laugh: keep moving, don't wait for it.)*

---

**[THE RELATIONSHIP]**

"But seriously — [Name] has been [one true sentence about what they are to you: 'my best friend since we were kids' / 'the person I call first, for everything' / 'my daughter, and my favorite person to argue with']. And if you know [him/her/them], you know [one quality everyone in the room will recognize: 'they would give you their last dollar' / 'she's the most stubborn person alive, and it's her best quality' / 'he has never once been on time']."

---

**[THE ONE STORY]**

"Let me tell you one story that sums [Name] up.

[Tell it. 4–6 sentences. Set the scene in one sentence. What happened in two or three. What it showed about them in one. Example shape: 'A few years ago, we... And instead of..., [Name] ... And that's exactly who [he/she/they] is — the kind of person who...']"

---

**[THE TURN — where the room goes quiet]**

"And then [he/she/they] met [Partner's Name].

I'll be honest — the first time I saw them together, I knew something was different. [One true observation: 'He couldn't stop smiling like an idiot' / 'She talked about him the entire drive home' / 'For the first time, my daughter was fully herself with someone.']

[Partner's Name] — since you came into [Name]'s life, [one true change you've watched: 'I've watched my best friend become the happiest I've ever seen him' / 'the whole family gained someone we didn't know we were missing']. Thank you for that."

---

**[THE TOAST]**

"So if everyone would please raise a glass.

To [Name] and [Partner's Name] — may your love keep growing, may your laughter stay loud, and may you always be as happy as you are tonight.

To the happy couple!"

## Page 4 — If You're a Parent, Swap This In

Parents carry a different weight than friends. If you're the father or mother of the bride or groom, replace THE RELATIONSHIP and THE TURN with these:

**[THE RELATIONSHIP — parent version]**

"[Name] came into our lives on [birth date or 'a day I remember like it was this morning'], and from that day, [one true early memory in one sentence]. Watching [him/her/them] grow into the person sitting there tonight has been the honor of my life."

**[THE TURN — parent version]**

"When your child finds the right person, you know. Not because they tell you — because you watch them come home different. Lighter. More themselves.

[Partner's Name], the first time [Name] brought you home, [one true detail]. Tonight we're not losing [a daughter/a son]. We're gaining [you / a son / a daughter], and this family is better for it."

## Page 5 — The Delivery Card (print this, keep it in your pocket)

**The night before:**
- Practice out loud twice. Time it. Cut from the middle if over 5 minutes.
- Print the speech in large type, or put it in your phone notes with big font. Notes are not cheating. Rambling is worse than reading.

**One hour before:**
- One drink maximum before the speech. The second one is for after the toast.
- Eat something. Nerves on an empty stomach are worse.

**At the mic:**
- Stand still. Feet planted. The urge to sway is real — plant and stay.
- Speak 20% slower than feels natural. What feels too slow to you sounds right to the room.
- Look at the couple during the sincere parts. Look at the room during the funny parts.
- If a joke dies: keep moving. Never explain it, never apologize.
- The last line is the toast. Raise the glass, say the line, drink. Done. Don't add a bonus ending.
